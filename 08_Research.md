# Purpose

This document summarises the scientific foundations of habify30.

It is not intended to be a comprehensive literature review.

Its purpose is to document the research fields, theoretical concepts and empirical findings that have influenced product design.

Whenever possible, product decisions should be traceable to established scientific evidence rather than intuition or current trends.

---

# Design Philosophy

habify30 is evidence-informed.

This does **not** mean that every product feature has been experimentally validated.

It means that design decisions should be grounded in well-established behavioural science wherever possible.

Research informs product design.

Product design still requires judgement, iteration and practical testing.

---

# Core Research Question

The central question behind habify30 is:

> How can organisations increase the probability that learning becomes sustained behaviour in everyday work?

The product intentionally focuses on behavioural implementation rather than knowledge acquisition.

---

# Research Domains

The current product architecture draws upon several complementary disciplines.

---

# Behaviour Change

Behaviour change research provides the conceptual foundation of habify30.

Core assumptions include:

- Behaviour rarely changes through information alone.
- Behaviour is influenced by context.
- Repetition strengthens behavioural stability.
- Small behavioural changes are easier to maintain than large changes.
- Sustainable change requires implementation rather than intention.

These assumptions influence almost every aspect of the product architecture.

---

# Transfer of Training

Transfer research examines whether learning is applied after training.

One of the central findings is that successful learning does not automatically result in behavioural implementation.

Transfer is influenced by multiple factors including:

- opportunity to apply learning
- social support
- managerial support
- follow-up activities
- learner motivation
- organisational environment

habify30 addresses several of these variables directly.

---

# Habit Formation

Habit research strongly influences the product.

Important assumptions include:

Habits reduce reliance on conscious decision making.

Repeated behaviour in stable contexts increases automaticity.

Consistency is generally more important than intensity.

Behavioural repetition therefore becomes one of the primary design objectives.

---

# Behavioural Psychology

Behavioural psychology contributes several important concepts.

Examples include:

- reinforcement
- behavioural cues
- environmental influences
- implementation barriers
- behavioural repetition

The product deliberately focuses on observable behaviour rather than attitudes alone.

---

# Self-Determination Theory

Self-Determination Theory provides guidance regarding sustainable motivation.

Three psychological needs are particularly relevant.

Autonomy

Participants should experience ownership of behavioural change.

Competence

Participants should experience increasing confidence through successful implementation.

Relatedness

Behavioural change becomes easier when supported by meaningful relationships.

These principles support the inclusion of peer interaction.

---

# Reflective Practice

Reflection is treated as an intervention rather than documentation.

Research suggests that structured reflection can improve learning, awareness and behavioural adaptation.

habify30 therefore integrates repeated opportunities for reflection throughout the transfer journey.

Reflection should remain connected to future action.

---

# Behavioural Experiments

The product deliberately frames implementation as experimentation.

This approach reduces psychological resistance.

Participants become more willing to test new behaviours when success is not defined as perfection.

Experimentation supports curiosity rather than judgement.

---

# Identity Development

Repeated behaviour gradually influences self-perception.

Rather than attempting to directly change beliefs or identity, habify30 creates repeated behavioural experiences that may contribute to identity development over time.

Identity change is considered an emergent consequence of sustained behavioural implementation.

---

# Peer Learning

Social learning research suggests that people learn through interaction, observation and shared reflection.

Peer structures contribute through:

- encouragement
- accountability
- perspective
- normalisation of setbacks

habify30 therefore integrates peer interaction as a behavioural support mechanism rather than as a teaching mechanism.

---

# Cognitive Load

Behaviour change competes with everyday work.

Participants possess limited attention.

The product therefore attempts to minimise unnecessary cognitive load.

Examples include:

- one behavioural focus
- short interactions
- concise reflection
- minimal navigation
- progressive guidance

Reducing friction is considered a behavioural intervention.

---

# Psychological Safety

Behavioural experimentation requires psychological safety.

Participants should feel able to try, adjust and occasionally fail without negative consequences.

This principle influences:

- reflection design
- peer interaction
- behavioural experiments
- language
- facilitator guidance

The product intentionally avoids judgemental language.

---

# Everyday Context

Research consistently demonstrates that context influences behaviour.

habify30 therefore attempts to embed behavioural implementation directly into normal work situations.

The product avoids separating learning from reality.

Instead, reality becomes the learning environment.

---

# Research Principles Adopted

The following principles currently guide product development.

Learning does not equal implementation.

↓

Behaviour is observable.

↓

Small changes are sustainable.

↓

Reflection increases awareness.

↓

Repetition increases stability.

↓

Peers increase consistency.

↓

Autonomy supports motivation.

↓

Everyday work is the primary learning environment.

---

# Research Principles Rejected

The product intentionally avoids several assumptions that are common in traditional learning.

More content automatically creates more learning.

↓

Motivation alone creates behavioural change.

↓

One workshop creates lasting change.

↓

Completion equals success.

↓

High platform engagement equals high behavioural implementation.

These assumptions are considered insufficiently supported by behavioural evidence.

# Evidence Standard

Future product decisions should ideally satisfy one or more of the following criteria.

Supported by behavioural science.

Supported by practical evidence.

Supported by user testing.

Supported by repeated observation.

Features that satisfy none of these criteria should be viewed critically.

# Confidence

## Established

- Behavioural science forms the foundation of habify30.
- Transfer of learning is the central research domain.
- Reflection, repetition and peer support are evidence-informed design choices.
- Everyday work is the intended implementation environment.

## Working Assumptions

- Future research may refine the weighting of individual mechanisms.
- Additional evidence may improve measurement approaches.

## Open Questions

- Which behavioural indicators best predict long-term transfer?
- Which transfer interventions produce the highest return on effort?
- How can behavioural implementation be measured objectively in organisational settings?
