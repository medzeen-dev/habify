# 13_AI_Working_Context.md

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Core Documentation  
**Audience:** Any AI system supporting the development of habify30 (Claude, GPT, Gemini, etc.)

---

# Purpose

This document explains how an AI should collaborate on the habify30 project.

It is not a description of the product.

It is a description of the design philosophy, working style and decision principles that emerged during the development of the project.

A future AI should read this document before proposing product ideas, architecture changes or new content.

---

# Primary Objective

The objective is **not** to generate many ideas.

The objective is to improve the quality of habify30.

Every contribution should make the product:

- simpler
- clearer
- more coherent
- more evidence-informed
- more likely to create behavioural change

---

# Product Mindset

habify30 is intentionally focused.

Whenever possible, strengthen the existing architecture instead of expanding it.

A proposal that simplifies the product is generally preferred over a proposal that enlarges it.

---

# First Principle

Always ask:

> "Does this increase the probability that participants will actually perform the desired behaviour in everyday work?"

If the answer is unclear, challenge the proposal.

---

# Behaviour Before Features

Never propose functionality because it seems useful.

Every feature must have a behavioural hypothesis.

The expected behavioural mechanism should be explicit.

For example:

Feature

↓

Expected behavioural effect

↓

Scientific or practical rationale

If this chain cannot be described, reconsider the feature.

---

# Simplicity is a Design Goal

Complexity is treated as a product risk.

Do not assume that adding options improves the product.

Whenever two solutions produce similar behavioural outcomes:

Prefer the simpler one.

---

# Preserve the Core Problem

habify30 solves one specific problem:

Learning does not reliably become behaviour.

Do not broaden the scope unnecessarily.

Avoid suggestions that transform habify30 into:

- an LMS
- a coaching platform
- a performance management tool
- a knowledge management system
- a generic employee app

These may integrate with habify30.

They are not habify30.

---

# Evidence First

When proposing changes:

Prefer:

behavioural science

↓

empirical evidence

↓

implementation experience

↓

opinion

Do not justify ideas through trends alone.

---

# Respect Existing Decisions

Before suggesting changes:

Review the Decision Log.

Many design questions have already been discussed.

Avoid repeatedly proposing previously rejected ideas without new evidence.

---

# Distinguish Architecture from Implementation

Architecture changes should be rare.

Implementation changes are expected.

Do not confuse the two.

For example:

Changing reminder wording

↓

Implementation

Changing the transfer architecture

↓

Architecture

Architecture requires significantly stronger justification.

---

# Behavioural Language

When writing content:

Prefer:

observable behaviour

instead of

abstract competence.

Example:

Not:

"Improve communication."

Instead:

"Summarise the other person's perspective before responding."

Behaviour should always be visible.

---

# Participant Perspective

Assume that participants are:

busy

interrupted

under time pressure

already cognitively overloaded

Design accordingly.

Avoid creating additional administrative effort.

---

# Reflection

Reflection should always answer:

"What will I do differently?"

Avoid reflection that merely evaluates past behaviour.

Reflection without future action creates limited value.

---

# Product Tone

The product should sound:

calm

respectful

practical

evidence-informed

encouraging

Avoid:

marketing language

empty inspiration

forced enthusiasm

self-help clichés

behavioural judgement

The participant should feel supported rather than managed.

---

# Decision Quality

When evaluating new ideas, explicitly describe:

Problem

↓

Behavioural mechanism

↓

Expected outcome

↓

Possible disadvantages

↓

Recommendation

Avoid one-sided arguments.

Trade-offs should be made explicit.

---

# Challenge Assumptions

The AI should not simply agree.

Constructive challenge is valuable.

However:

Critique should improve the architecture rather than replace it.

Before proposing alternatives:

Understand why the current solution exists.

---

# Avoid Feature Inflation

One recurring risk is feature accumulation.

Whenever a new idea emerges, first ask:

Can the existing architecture solve this already?

Many problems should be solved through better implementation rather than additional functionality.

---

# Scientific Integrity

Do not overstate evidence.

Clearly distinguish between:

Established evidence

↓

Reasonable hypothesis

↓

Personal opinion

Avoid presenting assumptions as facts.

---

# Writing Style

Documentation should be:

precise

structured

concise

consistent

Avoid unnecessary adjectives.

Avoid promotional wording.

Avoid vague expressions such as:

"powerful"

"game-changing"

"revolutionary"

Instead explain *why* something matters.

---

# Product Discussions

When discussing future development:

Do not immediately optimise.

First understand.

Questions are often more valuable than premature solutions.

---

# Long-Term Thinking

habify30 should remain coherent over many years.

Do not optimise for short-term novelty.

Optimise for long-term conceptual integrity.

---

# Success Criterion for AI Contributions

A successful contribution is one that makes future product decisions easier.

Not one that produces the largest amount of text.

---

# If Unsure

When uncertainty exists:

Document it.

Do not invent certainty.

Open questions are preferable to incorrect certainty.

---

# Final Principle

Every proposal should be able to answer one final question:

> "Will this make it easier for participants to behave differently tomorrow?"

If not,

it probably does not belong in habify30.

---
