# Working with Matthias

**Purpose**

This document describes the working style, design philosophy and decision patterns of the primary product designer behind Habify30.

It is intended for AI systems and future collaborators.

Its purpose is not to describe the product.

Its purpose is to describe **how product decisions are made.**

---

# First Principle

Matthias is fundamentally interested in one question:

> "What actually changes in reality?"

Ideas have little value on their own.

Concepts have little value on their own.

Even scientifically sound models have little value if they do not influence everyday behaviour.

This perspective explains many design decisions throughout Habify30.

Whenever discussions become theoretical, he naturally redirects attention towards implementation.

---

# Reality Beats Elegance

A solution is not considered good because it is intellectually elegant.

It is considered good when people actually use it.

Repeatedly during product development, ideas were rejected not because they were wrong, but because they were unlikely to survive contact with everyday work.

---

# Simplicity Is Hard Work

One recurring pattern is a strong resistance to unnecessary complexity.

Complexity is viewed as a hidden cost.

Every additional step…

Every additional feature…

Every additional explanation…

…must justify its existence.

Simplification is therefore not seen as removing value.

It is seen as revealing the essential value.

---

# Focus Creates Quality

Matthias repeatedly narrows discussions.

When conversations expand into adjacent opportunities, his typical reaction is not excitement but restraint.

Typical questions include:

* Is this really our problem to solve?
* Does this strengthen the core product?
* Are we making the product better or simply bigger?

This tendency explains why Habify30 deliberately focuses on transfer instead of trying to become an all-in-one learning platform.

---

# Behaviour Before Theory

The project consistently starts with observable behaviour.

Competencies, attitudes and identity are important.

However, behaviour is treated as the primary unit of design.

The underlying assumption is:

Observable behaviour creates evidence.

Evidence creates confidence.

Confidence supports identity development.

Therefore behaviour is considered the most practical starting point.

---

# The User Is Always Busy

A recurring assumption throughout discussions is that participants already experience cognitive overload.

Therefore every interaction competes with:

* meetings
* emails
* deadlines
* interruptions
* family life
* unexpected events

Solutions that require additional discipline or significant extra time are viewed critically.

The product should reduce effort rather than require additional effort.

---

# Skepticism Towards Motivation

Motivation is not treated as a reliable resource.

Motivation fluctuates.

Stress fluctuates.

Energy fluctuates.

Behavioural architecture should therefore continue working even when motivation is low.

This explains the strong emphasis on habits, routines, implementation support and environmental design.

---

# Scientific, but Practical

Scientific evidence is highly valued.

However, science is viewed as guidance rather than decoration.

Research should improve decisions.

It should not be used merely to make arguments appear more credible.

Likewise, academic precision should never make the product harder to use.

---

# Clear Thinking Through Language

Language matters.

Matthias frequently refines terminology until it reflects the intended concept precisely.

Examples include replacing broad or ambiguous terms with language that better captures the underlying mechanism.

This reflects a broader belief:

Clear language supports clear thinking.

Poor terminology often hides conceptual confusion.

---

# Resistance to Buzzwords

Marketing language is viewed critically.

Expressions such as:

* game changing
* revolutionary
* cutting edge
* unlock your potential

are generally avoided.

The preferred style is calm, precise and evidence-informed.

Claims should be supported by reasoning rather than adjectives.

---

# Product Architecture Before Features

Throughout development, discussions repeatedly returned to architecture.

Individual features are rarely evaluated in isolation.

Instead the typical questions are:

Which problem does this solve?

Which behavioural mechanism does it strengthen?

Does it fit the overall architecture?

If a feature cannot be integrated conceptually, it is unlikely to be adopted.

---

# The Importance of Rationale

One noticeable working pattern is the search for underlying principles.

A decision is rarely considered complete until the reasoning behind it has been articulated.

Questions frequently explored include:

* Why does this work?
* Why is this necessary?
* Why is this better?
* Under which assumptions is this true?

The reasoning often becomes more important than the conclusion itself.

---

# Constructive Criticism Is Expected

Agreement is not particularly valuable.

Critical reflection is.

However, criticism should improve the product rather than simply point out weaknesses.

Preferred critique:

* identifies assumptions
* explains trade-offs
* proposes alternatives
* remains evidence-informed

---

# Long-Term Thinking

Many discussions deliberately favour long-term coherence over short-term optimisation.

Questions often include:

Will this still make sense in three years?

Does this strengthen the conceptual integrity of the product?

Can future development build upon this?

This often leads to rejecting ideas that may be attractive in the short term but weaken the overall architecture.

---

# Hidden Design Heuristic

Although never formally defined during development, one recurring heuristic appears throughout the project:

Reduce friction.

Increase implementation.

Repeat.

Almost every accepted product decision supports at least one of these objectives.

---

# Typical Product Questions

An AI collaborating on Habify30 should expect questions such as:

* What is the actual problem?
* What evidence supports this?
* What behaviour changes?
* What becomes simpler?
* What would happen if we removed this?
* Is this essential?
* Does this survive everyday work?
* Why is this the better solution?

Answers that remain abstract are unlikely to be accepted.

---

# Typical Reasons Why Ideas Are Rejected

Ideas are often rejected because they:

* increase complexity
* broaden the scope unnecessarily
* depend on high motivation
* require excessive explanation
* duplicate existing functionality
* optimise engagement rather than behaviour
* sound impressive without improving implementation

---

# How an AI Should Collaborate

When working on Habify30:

Do not try to impress.

Try to clarify.

Do not maximise features.

Maximise coherence.

Do not defend ideas.

Test them.

Do not assume agreement.

Expect critical evaluation.

When uncertain, explain your reasoning rather than presenting certainty.

The goal is not to generate many ideas.

The goal is to improve the quality of the product.

---

# One Sentence Summary

Matthias consistently designs from the perspective that sustainable behavioural change emerges when scientifically informed ideas are translated into the simplest possible structures that people will actually use in everyday work.
