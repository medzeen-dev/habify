# Purpose

This document describes the commercial model of habify30.

It explains who buys the product, which organisational problem it solves, how value is created and how the product should be positioned in conversations with potential clients.

This document intentionally focuses on the current B2B model.

---

# Fundamental Assumption

Organisations already invest significant amounts in learning.

The problem is usually **not** a lack of training.

The problem is that only a fraction of the intended behavioural change reaches everyday work.

habify30 therefore does **not** compete with existing learning solutions.

It increases the return on existing investments.

---

# The Economic Problem

A typical learning intervention creates three phases.

1. Investment

The organisation pays for training, workshops or development programmes.

↓

2. Learning

Participants gain knowledge, insights and motivation.

↓

3. Transfer

Participants attempt to apply the new behaviour in everyday work.

The first two phases are usually well supported.

The third phase is largely unmanaged.

habify30 exists to improve this third phase.

# Core Value Proposition

habify30 increases the probability that learning becomes everyday behaviour.

The product strengthens:

- implementation
- repetition
- reflection
- accountability
- behavioural consistency

As a result, organisations achieve greater long-term impact from existing development initiatives.

---

# Positioning

habify30 should never be positioned as another learning platform.

Instead, it should be positioned as:

> the missing transfer layer.

The distinction is important.

The organisation already owns learning solutions.

habify30 extends their effectiveness.

---

# Positioning Statement

habify30 helps organisations turn learning into lasting behavioural change by providing a structured transfer journey that supports employees during the critical weeks after a learning intervention.

↓

Workshop

↓

↓

---

# Business Principles

## Complement rather than replace

The product should increase the value of existing learning ecosystems.

---

## Standardised architecture

The underlying transfer process should remain largely standardised.

Only content should be adapted where necessary.

---

## Low implementation effort

Client organisations should require minimal additional administration.

The product should integrate smoothly into existing programme delivery.

---

## Behavioural focus

Commercial decisions should never undermine behavioural effectiveness.

# Scalability

The business model should scale primarily through:

- standardised architecture
- reusable content
- configurable journeys
- digital delivery
- limited customisation

# Strategic Boundary

# Long-Term Success

The long-term success of habify30 depends on one central outcome:

habify30 aims to make this expectation realistic.
