# Purpose

This document explains why habify30 exists.

It describes the underlying problem, the philosophy of the product and the principles that guide every product decision.

While the Project Summary explains *what* habify30 is, this document explains *why* it should exist.

---

# The Starting Point

Organisations invest billions every year in learning and development.

Employees attend workshops.

Managers participate in leadership programmes.

Teams complete online courses.

Experts share knowledge.

Participants often report that the training was inspiring, relevant and valuable.

Yet a simple question remains:

**What actually changes afterwards?**

For many organisations, the honest answer is:

**Much less than intended.**

---

# The Transfer Gap

The central problem habify30 addresses is the gap between intention and implementation.

During a workshop people often think:

- "I should listen more."
- "I want to delegate differently."
- "I should give feedback more frequently."
- "I want to protect more focus time."

These intentions are genuine.

However, everyday work quickly reasserts itself.

Emails accumulate.

Meetings return.

Deadlines dominate attention.

Existing habits take over.

The intended behaviour is postponed until it quietly disappears.

The result is not a lack of motivation.

The result is a lack of transfer.

---

# A Different Perspective

habify30 starts from a different assumption.

The primary challenge is not creating insight.

The primary challenge is helping people behave differently tomorrow than they behaved yesterday.

Learning creates possibility.

Behaviour creates value.

---

# Vision

To make sustainable behaviour change the normal outcome of organisational learning.

Learning should no longer end when the workshop ends.

Instead, every meaningful learning experience should continue into everyday work until the desired behaviour becomes part of normal professional practice.

---

# Mission

habify30 provides organisations with a practical transfer architecture that supports employees in translating learning into sustained behavioural change.

The product exists to increase the probability that important behaviours become everyday habits.

---

# Design Philosophy

Every design decision should answer one question:

> Does this increase the likelihood that the desired behaviour occurs in everyday work?

If the answer is unclear, the element should be questioned.

The product intentionally prioritises effectiveness over feature richness.

---

# Behaviour Before Knowledge

Traditional learning solutions primarily optimise knowledge acquisition.

habify30 optimises behavioural implementation.

Knowledge is considered a prerequisite, not the final objective.

The success of a learning intervention is therefore measured by observable behavioural change rather than participant satisfaction alone.

---

# Everyday Work as the Learning Environment

Real behaviour change does not occur during training sessions.

It occurs:

- during difficult conversations
- in meetings
- while making decisions
- under time pressure
- when priorities compete
- during collaboration
- in routine daily work

habify30 therefore treats everyday work as the primary learning environment.

The product accompanies people into reality instead of asking reality to resemble the workshop.

---

# Small Changes Create Sustainable Change

Large behavioural ambitions often fail because they create excessive activation energy.

habify30 deliberately focuses on small, repeatable behavioural experiments.

The objective is not dramatic transformation.

The objective is consistent repetition.

Repeated behaviour gradually becomes habitual behaviour.

---

# Reflection Creates Awareness

Behaviour often remains invisible to the individual performing it.

Reflection interrupts automatic behaviour.

By creating repeated opportunities to observe actions, thoughts and outcomes, participants become increasingly capable of intentional behavioural choice.

Reflection is therefore treated as a practical intervention rather than an academic exercise.

---

# Habits Instead of Motivation

Motivation naturally fluctuates.

Stress, workload and competing priorities influence commitment.

habify30 avoids relying on motivation as the primary mechanism for change.

Instead, the product helps participants create structures that make the desired behaviour easier to perform regardless of temporary motivation.

---

# Behaviour Change is Social

People rarely change behaviour in isolation.

Supportive social environments increase commitment, accountability and persistence.

habify30 therefore integrates peer elements where appropriate.

Peers are not supervisors.

They are learning partners who increase consistency through encouragement, reflection and shared commitment.

---

# Simplicity as a Product Principle

Behaviour change already requires effort.

The product itself should not.

Every unnecessary click, decision or complexity reduces engagement.

Whenever possible, habify30 should reduce cognitive load rather than increase it.

Simplicity is treated as a behavioural intervention.

---

# Supporting Autonomy

The product should never attempt to control people.

Participants decide:

- which behaviour to strengthen
- how to apply it
- when to practise
- how to reflect on progress

habify30 provides structure, guidance and support.

Ownership remains with the participant.

---

# Success Definition

Success is not measured by:

- course completion
- platform activity
- number of logins
- time spent

Success is measured by sustained behavioural implementation in everyday work.

Platform usage is valuable only insofar as it contributes to this outcome.

---

# Relationship to Organisations

habify30 strengthens existing learning investments.

It is not intended to replace:

- workshops
- facilitators
- internal academies
- coaching
- leadership programmes

Instead, it provides the missing bridge between learning and implementation.

---

# Long-Term Aspiration

The long-term ambition is that transfer becomes a standard component of organisational learning rather than an optional add-on.

Whenever an organisation designs a learning intervention, transfer should be considered from the beginning rather than after the programme has ended.

habify30 is designed to enable this shift.

---

# Product North Star

Every participant should be able to say:

> "I didn't just learn something new.

> I actually work differently today."

---

# Confidence

## Established

- Behavioural transfer is the central purpose of the product.
- Everyday work is the primary environment for learning.
- Small behavioural changes are preferred over large transformation efforts.
- Reflection, repetition and social support are core mechanisms.

## Working Assumptions

- The optimal balance between digital support and human interaction will continue to evolve.
- Some transfer elements may be adapted for different organisational contexts.

## Open Questions

- Which behavioural indicators should become the standard evidence of successful transfer?
- How should long-term behaviour maintenance be measured beyond the initial transfer period?
